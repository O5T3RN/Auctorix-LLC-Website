§02 — Approach

Two angles. One structure.

Every Auctorix mark is built from two triangles set against each other — the same way we build a brand: a steady foundation, and a sharper edge pushing through it.

64°

The foundation angle

Research, positioning, and message architecture. This is the wide base — the part of the work that doesn't move once it's set, because it's load-bearing for everything after it.

38°

The leverage angle

Campaigns, content, and channel execution. This is the sharper line — fast-moving, tested weekly, built to cut through a crowded feed without losing the foundation underneath it.

180°

The full picture

Reporting that ties activity back to revenue. No vanity metrics presented without the number they're supposed to move — every campaign closes the loop.

§04 — About

Built by someone who'd rather measure it than guess.

I'm Tyler Ostern, founder of Auctorix.

I started this studio out of Coos Bay, Oregon, because too much "marketing" is built on instinct that nobody ever goes back and checks. I wanted to run a shop that treats a campaign less like a piece of art and more like a structure — something you can test, measure, and trust to hold weight.

That's where the name comes from. Auctorix takes its root from the Latin auctor — an originator, the one whose authority a thing is built on. The mark is two triangles set at different angles, braced against each other: a wide, stable base and a sharp leading edge. That's the whole philosophy. Steady foundations. Sharp execution. Nothing left to luck.

Every engagement starts the same way — by figuring out what's actually load-bearing in your brand, and what's just decoration. Then we build from there.
